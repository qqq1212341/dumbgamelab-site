Fact #42

Bumpy ice helps these stones slide

Before curling games, droplets are sprayed onto the rink to form tiny ice bumps

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-22

Short answer Tiny ice bumps are part of preparing a curling rink. Properly prepared bumps reduce friction, but too many can slow the stone down.

You might expect a curling stone to need mirror-smooth ice to slide well.

Instead, before a game, workers spray water droplets over the rink. The droplets freeze into tiny bumps.

Are they messing up the ice before the game?

The stone travels over these bumps. That's why the ice gets prepped like this before every game.

The stone's whole base doesn't touch the ice. A hollowed-out bottom leaves a narrow ring of contact.

Properly prepared bumps reduce friction. But more isn't always better: too many bumps can slow the stone down.

I thought they were ruining it. They're setting it up.
