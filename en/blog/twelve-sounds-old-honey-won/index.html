Twelve squelch sounds lost to my old honey sound. Then I actually listened to it.

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-18

The footstep sound on the honey level of Wakppu Tower started life as a honey-pouring clip I pulled from a YouTube video and reworked. Over 8 rounds I tested 12 replacements from slime, mud and stomping videos, and none of them beat the old honey's similarity score of 6.8. When I finally listened closely, the winner sounded like a drum, so the same day I replaced it with one CC0 recording from Freesound, cut into 10 variations and left otherwise untouched.

Honey should sound sticky

Wakppu Tower is a mobile ASMR climbing game. You hop from one squishy platform to the next, and every landing makes a sound, so the footstep is the first thing your hands actually feel.

The honey level shipped on November 2. Its platforms are thick, sticky honey, and stepping on them needed that little peel when your foot lifts off.

My first honey sound was a honey-pouring clip taken from a YouTube video. I reworked it until it passed for a footstep and planned to find something better after launch.

8 rounds, 12 candidates, nothing above 6.8

Candidates had to be wet and sticky. Slime squishing, boots in mud, feet stomping on soft stuff: I pulled each one from a YouTube video and tested 12 of them over 8 rounds.

Each candidate went through a sound scorecard that gives a similarity score, and every score got compared against the honey sound already in the game. The old honey scored 6.8.

Zero candidates beat it. On November 9 I kept the old sound and only logged the results (PR #6412).

The winner was a drum

After logging the results I played the old honey sound again from the top, with earbuds in and nothing else running.

Boom. A drum.

My ears could have been wrong, so I ran it through a sound type classifier, and it came back drum hit 1.00 with no hesitation at all, which settled it.

So what did 6.8 actually mean? Only that the old honey was the least wrong of the bunch. If every candidate is far from honey, the top scorer is far from honey too.

Same day, new rule: one CC0 sound, used as is

On November 9 I changed the whole approach. Instead of ripping clips from YouTube and processing them into shape, I would pick one CC0 recording from Freesound and only trim it, fade it and set the peak to -4 dBFS.

For honey I chose «Gurgling And Squelching Sounds» by newlocknew, a CC0 recording of a squelching puddle (freesound.org/people/newlocknew/sounds/784955/).

That one file became 10 honey footsteps (PR #6447). The verdict from the first listen was one word: good.

Nine more ASMR sounds moved to Freesound CC0 the same day (PR #6456).

  • Ice, thin ice, ice tray, ice dropping out of the tray
  • Crushed can, can opening
  • Jelly, footsteps in snow, shaving foam

Two rules I keep now

First, ears before scores. A scorecard can only rank candidates against each other, and when the reference and the candidates are all wrong, the winner is wrong as well, so now every candidate gets one real listen before it gets a number.

Second, a CC0 sound used as is beats a YouTube clip I processed. The source and license are clean, and nothing breaks from stacking edits.

Twelve candidates and eight rounds, and the answer came from listening once. Slightly embarrassing. The honey level sounds like honey now, though.

FAQ

Where can I get free sound effects for games without copyright trouble?

I use Freesound with the license filter set to CC0. CC0 sounds can go into a commercial game with no attribution required. The same site also hosts sounds that need credit, so I check the license on every file.

Can I edit Freesound CC0 sounds before putting them in my game?

Yes, CC0 allows edits. I limited myself to trimming, fades and a -4 dBFS peak, and cutting one recording into 10 pieces gave me a full set of footsteps.

Can I use sound effects ripped from YouTube videos in a game?

Rights differ from video to video, so you have to trace every source. My old honey sound came from a YouTube clip; I have since replaced it with a CC0 recording whose source is clear.

Is a similarity score enough to pick a sound effect?

A score only ranks candidates against each other. My top-scoring honey sound came back from a classifier as drum hit 1.00. Listen once before you trust the number.
