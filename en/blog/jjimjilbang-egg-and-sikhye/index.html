Korean sauna eggs and sikhye: what a jjimjilbang actually sells

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-10-22

This is not a recipe, because search results for Korean sauna eggs and sikhye are almost all home recipes. It is a fact check of what a jjimjilbang (a Korean bathhouse and sauna complex) sells at its snack counter. The eggs are cooked for a long time until the outside turns brown, and are also called maekbanseok eggs. Sikhye is a sweet drink made by letting rice break down in malt water, and ordinary sikhye has no yeast, so it does not turn into alcohol. Prices vary by place, but 3 eggs in the 2,000 to 3,000 won range and a cup of sikhye in the 3,000 to 5,000 won range are common. Do not crack an egg on your forehead, which can hurt or bruise. Tap it on the floor or a table and peel it by hand.

What are jjimjilbang eggs and sikhye

The most common order at a jjimjilbang snack counter is a few roasted eggs and a cup of sikhye. After sweating in the saunas, people sit down and eat them as a snack.

In Korean the eggs go by several names: maekbanseok gyeran (맥반석계란), gu-un gyeran (구운계란) and gu-un ran (구운란), and some counters write hunje gyeran (훈제계란), smoked egg. Sikhye is a sweet drink made by letting cooked rice break down in malt water. Japanese renders it as シッケ and Traditional Chinese as 甜米露.

How are Korean sauna eggs different from boiled eggs

A boiled egg is cooked in water, while a roasted egg is heated for a long time and loses moisture. That turns the white brown and makes it chewier. English-language cooking blogs explain the browning as the Maillard reaction and say eggs made the same way at home come out a similar color.

By looks they come in three kinds: a roasted egg in a normal shell, a smoked egg with a brown shell, and a charcoal egg with a black shell. The English-language travel site Creatrip describes them this way.

Are maekbanseok eggs really roasted on stone

The name contains maekbanseok, a mineral stone, but not every egg is cooked on stone. A write-up of a 2011 MBC investigative show quotes a manufacturer saying only some factories roast eggs on maekbanseok and most use electric heat without it. That is a 15-year-old summary, so we cannot say it holds for every place today.

We found no public information on what machine a jjimjilbang counter uses. All we know is the food category: Korea's food safety ministry (MFDS) regulates roasted eggs as processed egg products.

Do not crack the egg on your forehead

Korean variety shows and dramas often show someone cracking an egg on their forehead. We found no solid explanation of where the habit started, only a personal answer saying TV made it stick. Treat it as folklore.

A roasted egg has a hard shell. A Japanese site of Korean-language guides and Creatrip both warn that cracking one on your forehead can hurt or bruise. Tap it on the floor or a table to make a crack, then peel it by hand.

Is sikhye alcoholic

The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture describes sikhye as made by pouring malt water over cooked rice and keeping it at 60 to 70 degrees for 4 to 5 hours. The enzymes in the malt turn the starch in the rice into sugar. To become alcohol you need yeast that eats sugar, and malt has no yeast, so ordinary sikhye stops at being a sweet drink.

Some things with similar names are different. Gamju and dansul are used for the same drink in some regions, and there are alcoholic variations such as yeonyeop sikhye, which uses sake with lotus leaf. Andong sikhye is a separate drink with radish and chili powder that is fermented without boiling. Makgeolli is fermented with nuruk and has roughly 5 to 8 percent alcohol, so it is not the same as sikhye.

How many calories are in a can of sikhye

For canned sikhye, the product label is the standard. Paldo's Bilak Sikhye (비락식혜) 238 ml can lists 90 kcal, 22 g carbohydrate and 19 g sugars on the official Paldo site. A shop selling the same product lists 85 kcal and 17 g sugars, so figures vary by period and volume, and checking the label before you buy is the accurate way.

Sikhye boiled on site at a jjimjilbang has no label. All you can say is that it is a sweetened drink. A Japanese guide site advises that drinking cold sikhye all at once right after sweating can shock the stomach, so drink water alongside it.

Is sikhye halal

The question "is sikhye halal" comes up often in Indonesian and Vietnamese searches, and it shows up in English autocomplete too. The usual explanation is that it is a drink that does not turn into alcohol, but halal status is decided product by product through certification.

We confirmed one fact. Food trade press reported that in 2023 Paldo received halal certification from Indonesia's halal authority (BPJPH) for 5 beverages including Bilak Sikhye, and began exporting to Indonesia in February 2024. We could not confirm whether other brands, or sikhye boiled on site at a jjimjilbang counter, are certified, so for those the only way is to ask.

Why are eggs and sikhye a pair

We found no certain origin. One explanation says the boiled eggs and drinks sold at neighborhood bathhouses carried over into the roasted eggs and sikhye of the jjimjilbang. There are accounts that sweet sikhye washes down salty eggs, and a Taiwanese immigration agency guide says they are good for refilling fluids and nutrients after sweating, but no scientific basis is given.

So this article says only "it has been eaten this way for a long time." A pairing that cannot be pinned on one reason has simply lasted.

How it works in our game

In Korean Spa Tycoon by Dumb Game Lab, the kiosk and the Sikhye Machine are separate facilities. You cannot place both from the start. The kiosk opens at 20 total guests, and the Sikhye Machine opens when you own two kiosks. You sell eggs before you can sell sikhye.

The prices differ too. A kiosk costs 1,500 won to place and a Sikhye Machine 4,000 won, and each use brings in 150 to 400 won at a kiosk and 300 to 700 won at a Sikhye Machine. These amounts are in-game money, not real jjimjilbang prices. A Sikhye Machine next to a Kiln Sauna sells 1.2 times as much, next to a Salt Room 1.15 times, and next to an Ice Room 1.1 times. A kiosk next to a Kiln Sauna also sells 1.2 times. We turned the real-life order of buying something right after sweating into a rule.

Where our game falls short

Korean Spa Tycoon is new, so it has few installs and almost no ratings yet. The eggs in the game only get peeled, and no one cracks one on their forehead. Sikhye is just a cup coming out of a machine, so the taste and nutrition details above are not something the game teaches.

In-game prices and multipliers are values set to balance the game. They do not reflect the real cost or profit of a jjimjilbang.

FAQ

Are Korean sauna eggs the same as boiled eggs?

No. A roasted egg is heated for a long time, loses moisture, turns brown in the white and is chewier. Korea's food safety ministry classifies roasted eggs as processed egg products.

Are maekbanseok eggs really roasted on stone?

Only some. A write-up of a 2011 TV report says most factories roast without maekbanseok, using electric heat. We found no public information on how jjimjilbang counters cook them.

Is sikhye alcoholic?

Ordinary sikhye is not. Malt has no yeast to make alcohol, so the rice only breaks down into sugar. Variations such as yeonyeop sikhye, and makgeolli, do contain alcohol.

Is jjimjilbang sikhye halal?

It depends on the product and the counter. The one confirmed fact is that Paldo's Bilak Sikhye received Indonesian halal authority certification in 2023. We could not confirm sikhye boiled on site.

How much do jjimjilbang eggs cost?

It depends on the place, and they are usually sold in 3s. The sources we found run from 2,000 won to 3,500 won for 3. Sikhye is commonly 3,000 to 5,000 won.
