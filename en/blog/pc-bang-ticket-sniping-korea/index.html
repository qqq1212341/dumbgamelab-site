Why Koreans Go to a PC Bang for Concert Ticketing

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-20

Koreans head to a PC bang (Korean internet cafe) on ticketing day because it rents out a seat with wired internet, a big monitor, a mouse and a keyboard, cheaply and by the hour. A PC bang does not move you up the queue, though. The ticketing site's server assigns queue numbers, and we found no measurements showing that a PC bang beats home. For foreign fans, a Korean-only kiosk, prepaying for time, Korean phone verification and fan club presale eligibility are bigger walls than the line speed.

How we checked

On November 20, 2026 we opened and read articles from a PC bang trade paper, reports from Yonhap News and JoongAng Ilbo, the NOL Ticket usage policy, Korean blog reviews, an r/kpophelp thread on Reddit and three Taiwanese blog posts, then checked them against each other. Where a story rests on a single source we say so. Anything we could not find is marked "can't confirm". The pieces we read came out between 2017 and 2026.

We are the team behind PC Cafe Tycoon (PC방 키우기), a game about running a PC bang, and every picture here is a screen from that game. None of them are photos of a real shop. We could not measure line speeds ourselves either, so you won't find numbers like "a few seconds faster" in this article.

Where "PC bangs are faster" comes from

In a November 2025 article, the PC bang trade paper I Love PC Bang (아이러브PC방) wrote that "it is well known that PC bangs, with fast and stable internet, have the edge." Its December piece said PC bang connections are "more stable and consistent than home lines," so shops fill up when a famous singer's concert, the pro baseball postseason or university course registration opens. A trade paper leans toward the shops' side. Still, the belief has clearly spread across Korea.

You can see it in the search box. On November 20, 2026, typing "티켓팅 pc방" (ticketing PC bang) into Google brought up the suggestions "why do ticketing at a PC bang" (티켓팅 pc방에서 하는 이유) and "ticketing PC bang or home" (티켓팅 pc방 집), while the Traditional Chinese "韓國網咖" (Korean internet cafe) showed "搶票" (grabbing tickets) on the second line. Koreans ask "why a PC bang?" as well. We can't confirm search volumes.

The server hands out your queue number

The queue is a virtual line that lets the crowd in a few at a time when booking opens, and the ticketing site's server assigns the numbers no matter where you connect from. What sets your number is the moment you press the button. One blogger got a number in the 7,000s on a 5G phone in June 2025, still landed tickets, and wrote that "the device you know best is the best one."

Speed claims point both ways. Internet installation agency Ajungdang (아정당) wrote that website waiting times barely differ between 100M, 1G and 10G plans, though that comes from a company selling internet lines. A personal blog argued that latency matters more than bandwidth and that a crowded PC bang can even get slower, with no measurements to show. On Reddit, one fan wrote: "Turns out the wifi at my place had a much better connection than the PC bang. I'd say go to a PC bang and test it out beforehand."

PC bang owners won't promise anything either. One owner in Jeonju wrote on their blog that anyone claiming one place is unbeatable "is a con artist" and that "honestly, it's just luck." Of the 7 to 9 people who came to the shop that day, 2 went home empty-handed.

What a PC bang actually gives you

The upside reviews keep returning to is the equipment, more than the connection. Replies on r/kpophelp said "If it's NOL, the mobile interface is absolute trash. Do it on a computer." and "the pop ups and the i'm not a robot verification checks do not work well with mobile. go to the PC bang." So you are renting wired LAN, a big monitor, a mouse and a computer that can run several windows, at the regular rate I Love PC Bang gives of 1,000 to 1,500 won an hour. For anyone without a decent PC at home, that is the biggest reason to go.

The same Jeonju owner suggested keeping four browsers open (Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Whale) and refreshing them in turn from 55 seconds on the server clock. The post also promotes the owner's shop. Meanwhile the person who wrote up a PC bang near Isu Station said the shop computer's browser was out of date and the mouse was sluggish, which made them nervous, so good equipment is no sure thing.

A PC bang on ticketing day

A blogger who went to a PC bang near Isu Station on the day of an IU concert presale in January 2024 wrote that "as it got close to 8, the place was half people gaming and half people waiting to buy concert tickets," and that 13 of the 20 people in their row were ticketing. Their queue number was in the 3,000s. They got tickets on two of the four days.

The trade paper also wrote that when bookings open for concerts by singers like IU or Lim Young-woong, walk-in customers who come only to buy tickets rise sharply. We found no statistics on how full PC bangs across the country get on ticketing day. The scene above is one shop on one evening.

Foreign customers turn up too. In a September 2025 Yonhap News report, a staff member at a PC bang in Jonggak said, "They game too, but many foreigners come in for things like ticketing, and the number keeps growing." The same report cited an analysis by the Shinhan Card Big Data Research Center showing that foreign tourists' PC bang use from January to July was up 36% on the year before. That 36% covers every kind of visit and does not single out ticketing.

Where foreign fans get stuck

For foreign fans, the shop entrance and the ticketing site are higher walls than the PC bang's connection. Yonhap reported that PC bangs usually have you prepay or sign up at a kiosk, and that most of those machines support no language other than Korean. One guide (BeautiPin, August 2026) says signing up needs Korean phone verification, while a 2017 Taiwanese blog said any number went through, so it is safer to assume it varies by shop.

Here is ticketing day, step by step.

  • Check the opening time in Korea time (KST). Plenty of NOL presales have opened at 8 p.m., but it differs by show
  • Arrive about an hour early to get a seat. That is how early the Jeonju PC bang owner recommends
  • At the kiosk, choose member or guest (비회원) and prepay for time. The computer shuts off when your time hits zero, so add plenty
  • Keep a server time site (Navyism and the like) open. Your device clock and the ticketing site's server clock can differ
  • Check the Korean/English toggle (한/영) before you type the captcha. The NOL captcha uses English letters and PC bang keyboards use the Korean layout
  • Read the show notice first for identity verification, a Korean phone or fan club presale eligibility. If you get stuck there, a PC bang won't help
  • Anyone under 19 can't be in a PC bang from 10 p.m. to 9 a.m. An 8 p.m. sale is not affected

Macros, proxy booking and multiple computers

Article 3 of the NOL Ticket usage policy lists abnormal repeated access through a program, getting around the system to reach the booking page before it opens, and booking in bulk over and over then canceling as grounds for limiting or canceling bookings. Article 4 allows only payment methods in your own name. We found no line that stops a person from opening several browser windows.

Plenty of blog posts say that however many computers you switch on, one account still gets one queue spot. The NOL FAQ list includes the question "Can I get multiple queue numbers by using several devices or browsers?" but we can't confirm the official answer itself. Borrowing a friend's account or handing the job to a "mercenary" (용병) who books for you runs into the own-name rule.

Macros get investigated. According to a JoongAng Ilbo report from October 2025, the Daejeon police cyber investigation unit caught three people who used macros at PC bangs in Seoul and Gyeonggi to book 100,881 pro baseball tickets and resell them, and in July 2025 officers made an arrest in the act at a PC bang in Yeoju with three computers running. Since August 28, 2026, the amended Performance Act and National Sports Promotion Act have treated every ticket resold above face value as a scalped ticket. Penalties go up to 50 times.

FAQ

Is ticketing at a PC bang really faster?

We found no measurements showing a PC bang is faster than home. Korean trade papers and reviews share the belief that wired means stable, and other reviews say home wifi did better. Most advice favors using the device you know best.

Will going to a PC bang move me up the queue?

No. The ticketing site's server assigns queue numbers, and where you connect from plays no part. The moment you press the button decides your number.

Can foreigners do concert ticketing at a Korean PC bang?

You can use a seat once you prepay for time at the kiosk, though reports say most kiosks only support Korean. The bigger walls are the ticketing site's identity verification, a Korean phone number and fan club presale eligibility. Check the show notice first.

Is the NOL captcha in Korean or English?

The NOL (formerly Interpark) captcha uses English letters. PC bang keyboards use the Korean layout, so check the 한/영 (Korean/English) toggle before you type.

Do more computers raise my odds?

Many reviews say one account gets one queue spot, so several computers on the same account may not add any lines. We can't confirm NOL's official answer. Someone else's account or proxy booking runs into the own-name rule, and macros are blocked by the terms and by law.
