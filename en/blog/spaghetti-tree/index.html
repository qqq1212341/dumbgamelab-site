The BBC Once Said Spaghetti Grows on Trees

On April Fools' Day 1957, hundreds of Britons called to ask how to grow a spaghetti tree

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-05

Short answer On April Fools' Day 1957 the BBC aired a report of a Swiss family harvesting spaghetti from trees, and hundreds of viewers asked how to grow their own.

Where does spaghetti grow? In 1957 Britain, plenty of people had no idea.

Back then spaghetti was an exotic food in Britain. Many people didn't know it's made from flour and water.

On April 1 that year, the BBC current-affairs show Panorama aired a three-minute report. It showed a family in southern Switzerland harvesting spaghetti from trees.

The film showed a harvest festival in Ticino, in southern Switzerland. The picturesque scenery made it more believable.

The voice-over was by respected broadcaster Richard Dimbleby. He is said to have gladly lent his authority to make the joke work.

If a trustworthy guy says it, you just believe it

An estimated eight million people watched. The next day hundreds phoned in to question it or ask how to grow their own.
