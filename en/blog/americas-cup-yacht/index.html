Fact #48

The “America” in the America's Cup Was a Boat, Not a Country

The trophy was a silver pitcher with no bottom, and it was first called the 100 Sovereign Cup

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-28

Short answer The America in the America's Cup is the name of the yacht that won in 1851.

The yacht racing event, the America's Cup. The “America” in that name isn't a country.

It's the name of one yacht that won in 1851. That boat was the America.

The America won a race around the Isle of Wight, off England.

I thought it was a country. It was a boat?

The prize at stake back then was the 100 Sovereign Cup.

The cup was a silver pitcher with no bottom. It weighed 134 ounces and stood 27 inches tall.

Back then, a cup was a prize the winner took home.

The winning American team took the cup home with them. There was even talk of melting it down to make souvenirs.

In 1857 the cup was donated to the New York Yacht Club.

The first year a race was held with this cup as the prize was 1870. It has been called the America's Cup since then.
