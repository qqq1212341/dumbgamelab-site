Fact #41

There's a reason this caterpillar flings poop

Flinging poop far from home can help hide a caterpillar's address

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-21

Short answer One leaf-sheltering caterpillar flings its poop away. The smell can guide hunting wasps to it, so removing the clue can reduce the risk of discovery.

A caterpillar folds a leaf into a home. It sticks its rear end outside and flicks a pellet of poop away.

Researchers studied why this caterpillar species removes its poop. Is it simply a neat housekeeper?

Does the trash really need to go that far?

Experiments with this caterpillar showed that the smell of its poop attracted wasps that hunt caterpillars.

In a separate experiment, caterpillars near poop also faced a higher risk of being eaten.

Poop left outside the home acts like a sign saying, 'Caterpillar lives here.' Flinging it away can remove that clue.

Not taking out the trash. Erasing the address.
