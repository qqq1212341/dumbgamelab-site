Jjimjilbang Etiquette: What Not to Do and What Confuses Everyone

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-10-16

You wear nothing in the gender-separated baths and the provided uniform in the shared sauna area. Shower before you enter any bath, and keep your phone and camera out of the changing room and baths. Tattoo rules are set by each facility, not by law, so ask at the front desk before you pay.

Do you wear underwear?

This is the first place people freeze. In the gender-separated baths and changing rooms you wear no underwear and no swimsuit. Guides from different sources all say the same thing.

In the shared sauna area, where you wear the uniform from the front desk, it's a different story. Whether you wear underwear under the uniform is up to you. You sweat a lot, so bring a spare pair for when you leave.

Can you go in with a tattoo?

Turning people away for tattoos is a facility policy, not a law, so it differs from place to place. A 2022 Korea Herald article reported a Seoul spa posting a notice of no admission for guests with tattoos that displease others, and said public bathhouses are no different.

One guide says old neighborhood baths tend to be conservative while newer large spas are looser. You can't tell which one you're at until you arrive, so if you have a visible tattoo, call ahead or ask at the front desk before you pay.

Where can you use your phone?

Keep it away in the changing room and the baths, and never take photos there. Someone undressed can end up in your frame, which is why places are strictest about it.

In the shared sauna and lounge areas, phones are fine. Keep your voice low and use earphones for video.

What to do before you get in the bath

Showering matters most. You wash and rinse at the washing stations before you enter a shared bath, and places expect this from the door.

  • Wash and rinse your body before you get in
  • Tie up long hair so it stays out of the water
  • Keep your towel out of the bath water
  • Don't get into the bath wearing the uniform
  • Don't eat in the baths
  • Keep your voice down. No loud calls or chatting in the baths

What confuses people in the kiln and sauna rooms

A kiln sauna (bulgama) is a room you go in and out of for a few minutes at a time. If you feel dizzy, step out and cool down in the ice room or the lounge. Travel guides also say to avoid the sauna after drinking alcohol.

In the shared area you keep the uniform on at all times. Even when it's hot, you take clothes off only when you go to the baths.

Sleeping room and lounge manners

The sleeping room is dimly lit, so keeping your voice low is the baseline. Guides keep coming back to the request for quiet.

Put big bags in a locker. Many places let you charge snack bar and restaurant purchases to your locker key and pay at checkout, so losing the key causes trouble.

FAQ

Do you wear underwear in a jjimjilbang?

Not in the baths or changing rooms, where there's no underwear and no swimsuit. In the shared sauna area you wear the uniform, and underwear under it is your choice.

Can you go to a jjimjilbang with a tattoo?

It depends on the facility, since it's a house policy rather than a law. If you have a visible tattoo, ask at the front desk before you pay.

Can you use your phone in a jjimjilbang?

In the shared sauna and lounge areas, yes. Keep it away in the changing room and baths, and never photograph anyone.

Do you have to shower before the bath?

Yes. Washing and rinsing your body before you get in is the most basic rule.

Can you take a towel into the bath?

The basic rule is to keep it out of the bath water. Put it on your head or leave it outside the bath.
