Fact #46

Blood-Red Water Flows From Antarctic Ice

The water is clear when it comes out. It turns red only after it meets air

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-26

Short answer The red water flowing from an Antarctic glacier is clear when it comes out. Beneath it live microbes thought to have survived for at least 1.5 million years.

Taylor Glacier, Antarctica. Red water runs down from a crack in a white wall of ice. It's called Blood Falls.

In 1911, the Australian geologist Griffith Taylor found it while exploring this glacier.

Ice is bleeding? What is going on in Antarctica?

But if you scoop up water just as it leaves the crack, it's clear. The red comes from iron dissolved in the water, which oxidizes when it meets air.

The water is brine that rises from beneath the glacier.

This brine contains living microbes. They live where there is no sunlight or oxygen, on chemical energy from iron and sulfur compounds.

Researchers think ocean-like microbes have lived under the glacier for at least 1.5 million years.

Sources

  1. SICB: Blood, brines and microbiology beneath Antarctic glaciers
  2. Mikucki & Priscu 2007, Appl Environ Microbiol (PubMed)
