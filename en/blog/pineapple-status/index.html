Pineapples Weren't Bought to Be Eaten

In 18th-century England, a pineapple was something you showed off at parties

Justin Lee, Dumb Game Lab · 2026-11-25

Short answer One estimate puts the cost of growing a single pineapple in 18th-century England at about the price of a new coach. They were for showing off at parties more than for eating.

One historian estimates that growing a single pineapple cost about as much as a new carriage.

One fruit for the price of a carriage? I'd rather just buy one.

The finest pineapples spent weeks going around evening parties in the city.

Eating came last. It was reportedly cut up only when it had started to rot and the smell had spread through the house.

The top of this building in Dunmore, Scotland, is one giant pineapple.

Pineapples mean hospitality, right? Apparently not.

The story that colonial American captains put a pineapple at the door to welcome guests doesn't appear in records from the time. It spread in the 1930s, when old mansion museums were trying to bring back the colonial era in an idealized form.
